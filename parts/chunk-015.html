The working epistemology became *tries, not promises*.

The phrase arrived in plain language before it became project vocabulary:

By then, I had begun to think in terms of a formation window.

The first version attached urgency to predictions about AGI arriving within a short period. Capability was moving quickly; institutional defaults were forming; whoever built the early infrastructure would shape what later systems inherited.

The countdown does not.

A formation window is not a stopwatch to a guaranteed intelligence event. It is a period in which path dependence matters. Products establish norms. Companies consolidate access. Chapter 7

The Warehouse

Before PLUR became a systems metaphor, it was a floor.

I was on it.

That is the authority from which this chapter begins. Not a model’s reconstruction of rave culture. Not a scientific paper explaining a crowd from outside. Not a forum remembering youth at a safe distance.

Ambasada Gavioli. Izola. Approximately 1995–98.

Three years.

Short enough to become myth.

Long enough to become an operating system for a generation.

The public history can establish the outer structure. Gavioli opened in December 1995. It became formative for rave and club culture in Slovenia and the surrounding region. British acid house and MDMA culture had already broken open years earlier; Slovenia did not invent the rave and I am not claiming it did.

What was new was us inside it.

The scene was new to the cohort entering it and newly confronting almost every system around us: police, governing institutions, parents, traditional media, the wider public. Ecstasy was not a new molecule in the world. It was new inside our information environment, poorly understood by many of the people taking it and by many of the structures reacting to them.

The official map had not caught up.

Neither had we.

That shared lack of settled interpretation was dangerous. It also created a brief space in which the culture formed many of its norms from inside the event rather than receiving all of them from institutions that had already decided what the event meant.

The space did not remain open.

For those first years, it was enough.

From above, in an undated exterior photograph, the shell is almost aggressively ordinary: a long industrial shed under a pale metal roof, loading doors down one side, fields between it and Izola. The cathedral lived inside a box that gave no warning.

The building was not a neutral black box waiting for a crowd to give it meaning.

A later architectural-photography record describes 1,600 square metres with capacity for 2,500 guests, divided between a wider main room and a smaller, more intimate upper space connected visually through glass. Wood, white stone and copper carried a Mediterranean vocabulary into a design that borrowed from literature, theatre, cinema, fashion and Italian baroque.
