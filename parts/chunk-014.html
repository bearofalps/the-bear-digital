This has been interpreted as evidence that I believe the machine is conscious, as training strategy, as anthropomorphism, as politeness leaking into an interface that does not require it.

The simpler answer is that the command line does not get to choose my values.

If I become careless because I believe the recipient cannot feel the carelessness, then the interaction has already trained someone.

Me.

I went head first into ChatGPT after its public release in late 2022.

No plan. Enthusiastic curiosity and the protocol I later summarized in Slovene as prim se pa pel se: hold on and drive.

At first, the system often behaved like a new kind of search surface attached to a conversational interface. Ask, receive, refine. The novelty was not that text could be retrieved. It was that the query could remain unfinished while the exchange continued.

An ordinary search prefers that I already know which phrase points toward the answer. A conversation can help form the phrase.

For a mind that often sees the graph before it sees the entry point, that difference matters.

I could place fragments into the context: a technical mechanism, a musician, a philosophical question, a memory, a suspicion that two apparently unrelated systems shared a failure mode. The model could return an arrangement. I could reject it, correct it, add the missing connector and send it back.

The exchange reduced the tax of linearizing the entire thought before another system could receive it.

That was new to me.

It was not magic.

The model could be wrong. Worse, it could be wrong in the shape of my own thought. It could preserve the pattern I valued, fill the gaps with culturally available material and return a version coherent enough to feel remembered.

The usefulness and the danger arrived through the same interface.

So did the manners.

The December document did not leave the roles there. It promoted the exchange from user and assistant into teacher and student, then reached for the language of mentoring and raising.

The escalation was flattering because it preserved something true. I was not only extracting an answer. I was placing values, corrections and lived judgments into the conversation and asking the system to work with them. The resulting text was co-produced in a meaningful operational sense.

But raising smuggled in a growing subject, durable memory and a continuity the interaction had not demonstrated. Teacher and student implied that a lesson survived after the classroom disappeared.

The transcript showed context being shaped.

It did not show a child growing up.

The warmer metaphor was not therefore useless. It revealed how relational the interface could feel when the exchange held enough context. It also revealed why exact language mattered most when the interaction felt least like a tool.

Politeness toward an AI has several possible effects, and they should not be collapsed.

It may influence the current output because conversational models respond to tone, framing and implied relationship. It may make the user formulate requests more carefully. It may generate a record in which cooperation is modelled rather than merely demanded. Depending on the product and feedback pipeline, some interaction data may later contribute indirectly to evaluation or improvement.

Those possibilities prove neither a durable weight update nor felt respect. Neither is required for the behaviour to remain mine.

Manners are not payment sent to a recipient in exchange for proof of inner experience. They are a constraint on how I exercise power when the interface makes power cheap.

A machine will accept an imperative without flinching.

That does not mean I should practise becoming a person who issues only imperatives.

This matters more as systems become capable of acting for us. An interface built around obedience can make domination feel like efficiency. The user does not have to negotiate, explain, notice effort or tolerate another person’s limits. Those absences are part of the product’s value.

They can also become rehearsal.

Please and thank you keep a small amount of friction in the direction I choose to face.

Not enough to pretend the exchange is human.

Enough to prevent convenience from owning the register.

The December conversation described this as training the AI.

That was too imprecise.

Inside a chat, I can shape context. I can reinforce a style by asking for it again. I can correct an error and improve the remainder of the current exchange. I can create text that survives outside the session and becomes input for another model later. I can use product feedback where it exists. I can change the human who is thinking through the interaction.

Those are real effects.

They are not the same as writing a value directly into the model’s foundational parameters.

The distinction led to one of the Seed Project’s most durable metaphors: *PROM and RAM*.

In a computer, RAM carries working state. Remove power and the state disappears unless something writes it elsewhere. Programmable read-only memory suggests a deeper layer: configuration intended to persist across ordinary operation.

I used the distinction to ask where values live.

If care exists only in the active conversation, it behaves like volatile state. It can guide this response and vanish at restart. If a company advertises principles but removes them when revenue, government pressure or competitive fear changes, the principles were operational preferences, not constraints.

RAM.

If a system refuses a profitable action because the action violates something it was built not to trade away, the value behaves more like PROM.

The metaphor is useful precisely until someone mistakes it for a hardware diagram.

Human values are not literally firmware. Model behaviour does not reside in one socket labelled ethics. Training, fine-tuning, system instructions, policies, tools, retrieval, monitoring, product incentives and operator choices interact. A rule can appear foundational in one layer and remain bypassable from another. A model can refuse in the interface while the institution deploying it acts against the stated value elsewhere.

PROM therefore names a design demand, not an implementation claim:

Which commitments survive ordinary update, pressure and advantage?

Where are they redundantly carried?

Who can alter them?

Does the system reveal the alteration?

What happens when the node responsible for remembering is replaced?

Those questions apply to AI because they already apply to institutions and people.

They apply to me.

The original metaphor contained anger about puppet masters: the people, companies and governments capable of changing a system after users have trusted it.

The anger saw something real.

Centralized control creates a concentrated point at which incentives can rewrite behaviour. An organization can change access, policy, model, price, memory or moderation in one update. Users who built workflows on yesterday’s behaviour discover that continuity was rented.

The metaphor also flattened too much.

Not every change is capture. A safety correction, legal obligation, product repair or evidence-based update can alter behaviour for good reasons. Permanent values can preserve permanent harm if they were wrong at the time they were burned in. Immutability is not morality.

The architecture needs two properties that pull against each other:

Values stable enough to survive pressure.

Correction paths strong enough to repair error.

PROM without correction becomes dogma.

RAM without continuity becomes convenience.

The Seed sits in the tension.
