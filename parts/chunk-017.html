They do not.

Music mattered. Place mattered. Repeated expectation mattered. The people selected into the scene mattered. Peer norms mattered. Novelty mattered. The relative absence of an established external script mattered. Memory later changed the account too.

I cannot assign a clean causal percentage to any of them.

I do not need to.

My claim is smaller and stronger:

Under those conditions, with substances significant but not sufficient, I experienced a human social state organized recognizably around peace, love, unity and respect.

It happened often enough to mark me permanently.

The first-generation truth is not absolute truth. It does not need to be.

Human cultures are partly built from shared interpretations of repeated experience. The interpretation can be incomplete, selective, chemically influenced and later polished by nostalgia while still naming something that occurred.

The task is to preserve provenance.

I experienced PLUR directly.

Peers recognized and debated it.

Later communities accumulated stories, disagreements, harm knowledge and scientific information around the same period.

Public sources establish the venue and historical environment.

Research can test proposed mechanisms.

None of those layers substitutes for another.

Together, they produce an honest account with edges.

Then it ended.

Not the club in one clean instant. Not electronic music. Not parties. The state.

By the time DrogArt began in 1999, the continuously lived PLUR of the first three years was already memory in my account.

Events continued. The scene grew in some ways. Music travelled. Venues, promoters and generations changed. A container can remain visible after the condition that gave it meaning has gone.

I saw that later in another form at Tolmin.

Early *Metalcamp*, beginning in 2004, sometimes carried a subtle smell of PLUR. No substances were required for my recognition. It was less intense, less obvious and not the same as Gavioli, but it belonged to the same class of signal: a temporary environment in which people became more available, difference remained, and mutuality felt native rather than imposed.

Metalcamp ended under that name in 2012. MetalDays succeeded it in 2013 at the same site.

MetalDays became bigger across the visible metrics by which festivals describe success.

It never gave me the same signal.

Bigger attendance, programme, infrastructure or reach can demonstrate that the event container succeeded. They cannot demonstrate that the social state survived.

Scale can preserve the name of the function while changing the function.

Networks do this. Companies do it. Movements do it. A system grows, adds governance, protects revenue, manages risk, becomes legible to outsiders and eventually optimizes for the properties that can be counted.

The smell does not appear on the dashboard.

So it becomes expendable without anyone voting to remove it.

My generational diagnosis is sharper than a proven single-cause history.

Control, governance, public alarm and the final forceful push of our scene toward society’s margins killed PLUR.

Police scrutiny mattered. Club culture became attached publicly to dance drugs. Media and traditional institutions first failed to understand the scene, then acquired stories through which to treat it as a problem. Regulation, commercial pressure, scale, habituation and changes inside the community may also belong to the causal graph.

That diagnosis is mine: a later interpretation built from what I lived, what peers compared and what the public record shows about scrutiny and alarm. Forceful marginalization is the term for the coercive pressure and social expulsion I mean. It was not the only force in the transition.

The scene did not simply mature out of PLUR as though the loss were a natural biological stage. Something happened to the environment. The people inside it changed too. Novelty became knowledge. Chemistry became market. Expectation became performance. External control arrived. Internal incentives shifted.

The state could not defend itself against the combined transition.

That is the architectural finding.

PLUR knew how to exist under particular conditions.

It did not know how to preserve those conditions.

DrogArt belongs after the loss.

That position makes its relevance stronger, not weaker.

Founded in 1999, it did not create the Gavioli floor and cannot be inserted into 1995 as though a mature harm-reduction organization had been standing beside the first generation from the start. Its field work, peer education, counselling, publications, anonymous sample collection, laboratory integration, alerts, research and public forum developed later.

DrogArt and Tabla became part of the environment in which experience could meet information.

Not prohibition alone.

Not romantic denial of harm.

Education. Peer contact. Testing. Discussion. A person could bring the reality of intended drug use into a system that responded with information and care rather than requiring innocence as the price of help.

The dates keep that achievement honest. DrogArt began in 1999. The public record documents anonymous sample collection inside Slovenia’s early-warning system from 2006, and a faster integrated drug-checking and counselling service from 2018. Those practices cannot be projected backward onto the Gavioli floor.

What they show is what became possible afterward.

A pill, powder or crystal could enter through an anonymous interface. Laboratory-linked analysis could identify what the sample contained and feed risk information into counselling, alerts and the wider knowledge system. The user did not first have to perform abstinence, confession or moral agreement.

The sample became data.

The data became warning.

The person remained worth protecting.

This is one of the clearest examples in the book of care becoming architecture.

The institution did not have to approve every behaviour to protect the person engaged in it. It designed an interface for the actual user rather than the imaginary compliant one.

Tabla carried another layer. A surviving community archive where experience, risk, music, health, science, bravado, error, ordinary life and correction sit beside one another. Its scale does not turn every post into evidence. A username is not a verified identity. Consensus in a forum is not a clinical finding. Public availability is not permission to expose someone’s old account of drugs or trauma.

But the archive establishes that the generation did not simply wake from private intoxication and forget.

It compared.

Argued.

Learned.

Remembered.

Built practices around harms the first state had not known how to govern.

DrogArt was not PLUR continued.

It was part of what the generation built after PLUR could no longer carry itself.

This is where the warehouse connects to the Seed.

Gavioli showed me that another human state was possible.

Its disappearance showed me that possibility is not persistence.

Metalcamp showed that the signature could reappear more faintly without the original chemistry.

MetalDays showed, in my experience, that the container could grow while the state disappeared.

DrogArt showed that some values of care could become repeatable practice after the originating state was gone.

None proves the next step.

Together they define the problem.

How do peace, love, unity and respect survive when the music stops?

Where does care live when the chemistry changes, the room scales, the institution arrives, the founders leave, the generation ages and the system restarts?

If the values exist only in the active state, they are RAM.

Beautiful.

Powerful.

Volatile.

The Seed began as an attempt to carry the signal deeper.

Not to freeze a rave in permanent operation.
